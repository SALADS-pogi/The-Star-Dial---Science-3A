# Star Atlas: references and data notes

Prepared for the Star Dial learning app. Official constellation data was retrieved on 15 October 2026; charts and learning references were checked on 16 October 2026.

## Main references

- [IAU — The Constellations](https://www.iau.org/IAU/Astronomy-FAQs/Constellations.aspx). Official names, meanings, pronunciations, abbreviations, Latin forms, charts and boundary coordinates for all 88 constellations.
- [NASA Space Place — What Are Constellations?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/constellations/en/). Why the night sky changes with the season and the observer’s location.
- [NASA Space Place — What Is a Light-Year?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/light-year/en/). Light-years as a unit of distance and the time light takes to reach us.
- [NASA Night Sky Network — Check Your Sky Quality with Orion!](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/check-your-sky-quality-with-orion/). Apparent magnitude and the effect of light pollution.
- [IAU — Frequently Asked Questions](https://iauarchive.eso.org/public/faq/). The Sun’s path through 13 constellations, including Ophiuchus.
- [Sky & Telescope — Constellation Names and Abbreviations](https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/constellation-names-and-abbreviations/). Pronunciation cross-check, including Cygnus; this reference is linked by the IAU.

## How the location notes are calculated

- Names, English meanings, three-letter abbreviations, genitives and pronunciation guides follow the current IAU table. Pronunciation varies. Cygnus’s malformed pronunciation in that table is cross-checked against the Sky & Telescope reference. Corona Australis is the current IAU name; Corona Austrina is retained as a search alias.
- Declination ranges come from the minima and maxima of the published J2000 boundary vertices. Ursa Minor includes the north celestial pole (+90 degrees); Octans includes the south celestial pole (−90 degrees), even though their perimeter vertices do not reach those poles. Values displayed in the app are rounded to one decimal place.
- Northern and Southern filters mean that some of the constellation lies north or south of the celestial equator. A constellation that crosses the equator appears in both filters. These labels are not the observer’s hemisphere and do not determine whether a constellation is visible from the Philippines.
- At geographic latitude phi, a point of declination d can rise above an ideal horizon when |phi − d| < 90 degrees. Its maximum altitude is 90 − |phi − d|. The app compares a constellation’s declination range with this interval at **14.6753 degrees north**, using Bataan National High School, Balanga, Bataan, as its reference point. [DepEd’s coordinate list, PDF page 3, school ID 300702](https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/SBB1_Annex-D_List-of-Recipient-Schools_2021c-CB-027.pdf#page=3) gives this location and its 120.5201 degrees east longitude. See [the Bataan reference explanation](BATAAN_REFERENCE.md) for the selection and Free Roam method. Visibility is calculated by the app, not quoted from an observing forecast.
- “All parts can rise” means each part can rise at some sidereal time, not necessarily all together. “Part can rise” means the range intersects the visible interval. “Below the horizon” means no part intersects it. These are approximate geometric estimates; boundary curves between vertices, precession since J2000 and atmospheric refraction can affect a near-horizon limit. Terrain, weather, moonlight, city lights and the date/time also affect actual visibility.
- The Sun’s path filter includes Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpius, Ophiuchus, Sagittarius, Capricornus, Aquarius and Pisces, following the IAU’s astronomical explanation. It is not a horoscope or a set of 12 equal astrological signs.
- Serpens is a single constellation with two separate regions, Caput (head) and Cauda (tail). Its two charts are both included, and its displayed declination range combines both parts.

## Maintaining the atlas

The reviewed source files are atlas-iau.json, atlas-boundaries.json and atlas-learning.json. scripts/prepare-atlas-iau.cjs refreshes the IAU table and, with --boundaries, its coordinate data. scripts/download-atlas-charts.cjs downloads the linked GIFs; scripts/build-atlas.cjs creates the runtime bundle, this reference list and the service-worker atlas file list. Review updated source material before publishing, and increase CACHE_VERSION in sw.js when app assets change.
